## 1. The Core System: 3-Step Process

Every trade follows this exact sequence — no exceptions.

| Step | Action | Detail |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Step 1** | Identify the Key Level | Find the level on the **4-hour or 1-hour chart** with ≥2 weeks of repeated reactions |
| **Step 2** | Entry Confirmation | Wait for the **Break & Retest**: price breaks the level → pulls back → holds above/below it for 10–15 min |
| **Step 3** | Trade Management | Enter at the key level, scale out at 2–3R first target, hold runners to next key level |

> *"Every trade that we take must be based off of a historical level on the chart we are watching."*

## 2. Position Sizing — The 10/20/2 Rule

### The Core Rule

```
10% of account per position
20% max stop loss on the option contract
= 2% max account loss per trade
```

### Numerical Example

| Account Size | 10% Position | 20% Max Stop | Max $ Loss |
|-------------|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| $10,000     | $1,000      | $200        | $200       |
| $50,000     | $5,000      | $1,000      | $1,000     |
| $100,000    | $10,000     | $2,000      | $2,000     |
| $225,000    | $22,500     | $4,500      | $4,500     |

### Allowable Variance
- **Standard trades:** 10% of account
- **High-conviction A+ setups:** up to 15–18% of account
- **Hard cap:** Never exceed 20% of account in a single position
- **Beginners:** Stay at exactly 10% until consistently profitable

### Options Delta Calculation for Stop Loss Validation

The 20% stop rule is validated through entry quality, not an arbitrary exit:

```
Contract Stop % = (Delta × Underlying Risk $) / Contract Price

Example:
- Underlying risk = $1.00 (stop 1 point below level)
- ATM contract delta ≈ 0.50
- Contract loss ≈ $0.50
- If contract priced at $3.15 → stop = $0.50 / $3.15 = 15.8% ✓ (within 20%)
- If underlying risk = $2.00 → stop = $1.00 / $3.15 = 31.7% ✗ (bad entry)
```

> *"If we're hitting that stop-loss level and we are down 40–50–60%, that means your entry was terrible."*

## 3. Key Level Identification

### Time Frames for Level Selection

| Priority | Time Frame | Purpose |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| Primary | 4-Hour | Major supply/demand zones |
| Secondary | 1-Hour | Fine-tune entries and zones |
| Execution | 2-Minute | Entry timing |
| **Never use for levels** | 1–5 Min | Too noisy; not A+ |

### Level Validation Criteria

A level qualifies as tradeable if it shows:
1. **≥2 clear reactions** at the same price zone over the past 2–4 weeks
2. **Historical reactions going back further** (extend the line left to verify)
3. **Reactions on both sides** (rejected from above AND supported from below at different times)

### How to Build Zones (Not Lines)

1. Mark the highest wick and the lowest wick of the reaction cluster
2. Include adjacent highs/lows within 0.5–1% of the primary level
3. Width of zone = area where you would NOT be comfortable being long (or short)
4. Delete the middle lines; keep only the outer boundary

### Automatic Key Level Types (Always Mark These)

| Level Type | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| Previous Day High (PDH) | Mark every day |
| Previous Day Low (PDL) | Mark every day |
| Pre-Market High | Mark before open |
| Pre-Market Low | Mark before open |
| Overnight High / Low | Mark before open |
| CPI/FOMC/Earnings print high | Mark on data days |
| Weekly highs/lows | Mark on Sundays |
| Daily 20 SMA | Always visible |
| Daily 50 SMA | Always visible |
| Daily 200 SMA | Always visible |

### Daily 20 SMA Rule
> The daily 20 SMA is **the best pullback long entry** in an established uptrend. When price pulls back to the 20 SMA in an uptrend, it is the highest-probability long location.

### Daily 200 SMA Rule
> The daily 200 SMA is a **major institutional buy spot** across all equities. A break & retest of the 200 SMA is a macro A+ opportunity.

## 4. The No Trade Zone (NTZ)

### Definition
The range between the **pre-market high** and **pre-market low**. No trades are initiated inside this range.

### NTZ Logic

```
NTZ = [Pre-Market Low] to [Pre-Market High]

NO TRADE if: Pre-Market Low < price < Pre-Market High

TRADE ELIGIBLE if:
  - Price breaks ABOVE pre-market high → look for long B&R
  - Price breaks BELOW pre-market low → look for short B&R
```

### Why the NTZ Works
- Inside the range: no directional continuation expected
- Options require direction; choppy range = likely loss
- Outside the range: a break + retest offers high-probability continuation

### Application Example
Pre-market high = 5,662 on ES, pre-market low = 5,635:
- **NTZ = 5,635–5,662** → no trades
- If ES breaks above 5,662 and retests it → **long entry**
- If ES breaks below 5,635 and retests it → **short entry**

## 5. Entry Confirmation — Break & Retest

### The Exact Sequence

```
1. KEY LEVEL identified on 4H/1H chart
2. Price BREAKS above (long) or below (short) the level
3. DO NOT enter on the breakout candle
4. Wait for price to PULL BACK to the level
5. Wait 10–15 minutes of price action HOLDING at that level
6. Enter in the "battle zone" — AT the level, while it's sideways/holding
7. Stop loss = break BACK through the level
```

### Entry Timing

| When to Enter | When NOT to Enter |
|--------------|------------------|
| During the sideways hold at the level | On the initial breakout candle |
| When you see wick holds at the level | After price has run 2–3% from the level |
| When a green engulfing candle forms at the level | When trade "looks good" and already moving |
| When EMA cloud confirms direction | When still inside the NTZ |

### Bull Flag at Retest = Highest Confidence
When a bull flag forms right at the retest level, it is the strongest confirmation:
```
Breakout → Pullback → Tight sideways flag AT the level → Flag breaks up → LONG
```

### Fake Out / Liquidity Grab Recognition
- Price spikes above a level and immediately reverses → **NOT a valid B&R** (fake out)
- Wait for 2–4 candles holding **above** the broken level before entering
- If it fails back below within 1–2 candles, the setup is invalid — take no trade

## 6. The Reclaim Ripper Setup

The highest-conviction variation of the Break & Retest.

### Setup Mechanics

```
1. Price is BELOW key level (key level has been acting as resistance)
2. Price BREAKS BELOW a secondary support level → attracts new shorts
3. Price quickly RECLAIMS back above that support level
4. Shorts are now trapped underwater → forced to cover → creates squeeze
5. ENTRY: When the reclaimed level HOLDS as new support
6. TARGET: Back to the original key resistance above
```

### Why It's Powerful
- Trap mechanism: shorts entered on the break get squeezed out
- Double confirmation: level broke, reclaimed, AND defending
- Best setup after a "liquidity sweep" of a key low

### Example (from transcript)
> "Nasdaq breaks the previous day low at 22,800, then quickly reclaims back above. All shorts from below are now underwater. Once that level re-defends itself, the squeeze carries price back toward 23,100."

## 7. Stop Loss Rules

### Cardinal Rules

1. **Stop loss MUST be defined BEFORE entering any trade** — never after
2. Stop loss = the **key level invalidation point** (not a dollar amount or %)
3. The 20% option loss is a **consequence** of good entry, not a fixed exit trigger
4. If the market breaks your key level, the trade is **immediately invalid** — exit

### Stop Loss Placement

| Trade Type | Stop Placement |
|-----------|---------------|
| B&R Long | Below the retest level (key level you broke above) |
| B&R Short | Above the retest level (key level you broke below) |
| Reclaim Long | Below the reclaimed level |
| Swing Long | Below the previous day's low or below the daily 20 SMA |

### Never Do This
- **Never add to a losing position** to average down
- **Never move stop loss lower** because you don't want to take a loss
- **Never hold past invalidation** hoping for a reversal

> *"My lack of profitability was always led by a single blowup day... If I would remove all of those blowup days out of my P&L, I believe I would have gotten to profitability 10 times faster."*

## 8. Risk/Reward Targets

### Minimum Acceptable R:R

| Scenario | Min R:R to Enter | Action |
|---------|-----------------|--------|
| Standard B&R | 2:1 | Take trade |
| Tight channel (consolidating market) | 3:1 | Reduce size or skip |
| A+ setup (HTF + multiple confluences) | 2:1 minimum | Full size |
| Below 2:1 | — | Do not take trade |

### Profit Target Ladder

```
Entry (at key level, risk = R)
  → Target 1: 2–3R → scale off ⅓ of position
  → Target 2: 4–5R → scale off another ⅓
  → Runners: hold with EMA cloud or intraday trendline to next key level
```

### Calculating R:R on Options

```
R:R = (Target Level - Entry Price) / (Entry Price - Stop Level)
      measured in underlying stock points

Example:
Entry: $136.50 (ARM)
Stop:  $136.00  → Risk = $0.50
Target: $141.00 → Reward = $4.50
R:R = $4.50 / $0.50 = 9:1
```

### SPX Example (from transcript)
```
Entry: 5,258 (after B&R of 5,250)
Stop:  5,250  → Risk = 8 points
Target: 5,285 → Reward = 35 points
R:R = 35/8 = 4.4:1 ✓
```

## 9. Trade Management & Scaling Out

### EMA Cloud Rule (9/21 EMA)
- **9 EMA above 21 EMA** → green cloud → **stay long, hold runners**
- **9 EMA below 21 EMA** → red cloud → **stay short, hold short runners**
- When cloud flips color → consider exiting remaining position

### Scaling Out Protocol

```
Step 1: Get to 2–3R → sell ⅓ of position (book profit, reduce risk)
Step 2: Get to 4–5R → sell another ⅓ (now well ahead, can hold stress-free)
Step 3: Hold runners to next key level target, using EMA cloud as guide
Step 4: At the final target level → exit remaining ⅓–¼
```

### Options-Specific: Scale Into Every Pop
> "Every time ARM would pop to the upside, those option premiums expand in our favor. Scale out into those expansions."

### Day Trade → Swing Conversion Rule
A day trade can be held overnight **only if**:
1. At least ⅔ of the position has been sold at profit
2. The key level (stop level) has NOT been breached
3. The stock is still above the entry-level key support
4. Remaining position = small (10–15% of original)

### Maximum Hold: Next Key Level
Always know the **next key resistance level** before entering. That is the outer target.  
Example: Long from $136 on ARM → next level = $141.60 → that is the ceiling target.

### Intraday Trend Guide
Draw a simple uptrend line from the entry low. As long as price holds above the trendline and EMA cloud stays green, hold the position through small pullbacks.

## 10. Market Context Filters

### Expansion vs. Consolidation

| Market State | Identification | Action |
|-------------|---------------|--------|
| **Expansion** | Strong directional moves, B&R setups breaking and running | Full size, be aggressive |
| **Consolidation** | Channel between PDH and PDL, no breakouts, back and forth | Reduce size 50–75%, take base hits |
| **Chop** | Inside NTZ all day, fakeouts on every break | No trades; stand aside |

### How to Identify Consolidation
- Pre-market high and pre-market low intact all day
- Market oscillates between channel low and channel high
- No B&R setup produces >1R follow-through
- Action: *Do not force trades in a consolidating market. The B&R requires continuation.*

### Major Data Days (CPI, FOMC, PPI, Earnings)
- **Day before major data:** Expect lackluster, sideways action — reduce size or sit out
- **Day of data:** Expect volatility spikes — use wider NTZ; wait for initial move to settle before entering
- **Swing trade caution:** Do NOT hold swing trades through major macro events unless thesis is very clear

## 11. Leading Indicator — The SMH Rule

### Core Principle
The **SMH (VanEck Semiconductor ETF)** often leads the Nasdaq and S&P 500. Reading SMH before trading indices gives an edge.

### SMH Decision Tree

```
Is SMH at a key support level (gap fill, daily 20 SMA, previous high)?
  YES → Look for longs on Nasdaq/individual semis
  NO, still weak → DO NOT long the Nasdaq

Is SMH at a key resistance?
  YES → Be cautious of longs; watch for rejection
  NO, still strong → Longs on Nasdaq remain valid
```

### Specific SMH Signals
| SMH Signal | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| SMH fills daily gap + holds daily 20 SMA | Strong long signal on Nasdaq |
| SMH breaks to new high + holds breakout | Go long individual semiconductor names |
| SMH weak/rejecting while Nasdaq at support | Do NOT long Nasdaq |
| SMH daily 20 SMA reclaimed | Bullish for overall market |

### Market Driver Rule
> *"Identify what sector is driving the market right now. Watch that sector — not just the S&P and Nasdaq. The sector is the fuel. If the fuel is gone, the market won't bounce."*

Currently semiconductors → in the future could be financials, software, energy, etc.

## 12. A+ Setup Criteria (Higher Time Frame)

An A+ setup requires **ALL of the following**:

| Criterion | Requirement |
|----------|------------|
| Time frame | Weekly, 4H, or Daily — never intraday-only |
| Level | Major previous high or low (not a random pivot) |
| Structure | Higher lows forming after a pullback (bullish) OR lower highs forming after a rally (bearish) |
| Moving average | Daily 20 SMA or Daily 200 SMA within 2–3% of level |
| Confirmation | 10–15 min of price action holding the level on intraday |
| R:R | Minimum 3:1 to next key level |

### A+ Long Setup — Step by Step

```
Weekly/Daily chart:
  → Find previous ALL-TIME HIGH or major multi-month high
  → Price pulls back to retest that level
  → Look for HIGHER LOWS forming on 4H chart (structure shift)
  
4H chart:
  → Previous structure highs turning into lows (bullish flip)
  → Daily 20 SMA nearby (extra confluence)
  
1H chart:
  → Inverse head & shoulders pattern (optional but strong signal)
  → "Reclaim Ripper" potential
  
Intraday (2-min):
  → Break & retest hold for 10–15 min at the weekly/daily level
  → Enter long
```

### Examples of A+ Setups (from transcripts)
- Hood: Pulled back to weekly previous highs at $70 → 4H higher lows formed → ran to $120
- Nvidia: Pulled back to daily 200 SMA → higher lows on 4H → 100%+ return
- Google: Previous all-time high retest → inverse H&S → long for 100%+ on options

## 13. Pre-Market & Sunday Preparation

### Sunday Prep Checklist (in order)

1. **Macro Calendar** (Forex Factory)
   - Mark all red-folder events (CPI, PPI, FOMC, NFP)
   - Weeks with 3+ red-folder events → reduce swing trade exposure
   - Day before major event = likely consolidation day

2. **Earnings Calendar**
   - Mark all large-cap earnings for the week
   - Semis earnings (TSM, NVDA, AMD) affect SMH and overall market
   - Avoid swing trades through earnings unless well-managed

3. **Weekly Futures Analysis (NQ + ES)**
   - Start on 4-Hour chart
   - Mark the week's high and low
   - Create a supply zone (red) and demand zone (green) for the week
   - Define: "If we break X, the next level is Y"

4. **Watchlist** — 3 to 5 stocks max
   - Stocks with clean B&R setup visible on daily/4H chart
   - Set price alerts at key levels (do not watch all day)
   - Prioritize outperforming stocks in the leading sector

5. **Daily 20 SMA Check on Watchlist**
   - Best long opportunity = pullback to daily 20 SMA in an uptrend
   - If daily 20 SMA coincides with previous highs → highest priority watch

### Daily Pre-Market Routine (8:00 AM)

```
1. Adjust key levels from Sunday based on new price action
2. Mark overnight high and overnight low
3. Mark pre-market high and pre-market low
4. Define NTZ
5. Write out "if-then" statements:
   "If ES breaks above [pre-market high], I look for a B&R long"
   "If ES breaks below [pre-market low], I look for a B&R short"
6. Review SMH position relative to its key levels
7. Review any open swing positions for management
```

### Alert System
> *"I set an alert at $136. I simply wait for the alert to go off. I don't need to be watching this at every moment."*

- Set alerts at ALL key levels, not just the ones you expect to trade
- When an alert fires → evaluate if it generates a B&R setup
- If not → reset alert and wait

## 14. Scaling Up Your Trading

### Readiness Test (Before Scaling)
Answer "yes" to ALL before increasing size:
1. Comfortable in trades at current size? (no panic, no early exits)
2. Executing correctly at current size? (following rules, defined stop, defined entry)
3. Consistent for 2–3 months at current size?
4. Win rate and R:R positive over trailing 50+ trades?

### Scaling Protocol

```
Start: $500 position size → master it
Next:  $1,000 → master it
Next:  $2,000 → master it
Continue doubling until uncomfortable or execution degrades
If uncomfortable → scale BACK to previous level
Never skip more than 1 level at a time
```

### Account Growth Obligation
> *"If my account grows to $200,000 and I'm still taking $2,000 position sizes, I'm trading with 1% of my account. I am not going to see growth by doing that."*

Maintain the **10% position size as a fixed percentage**, not a fixed dollar amount.

### Scale Back Triggers (must scale down when ANY apply)
- Shaky executions, getting out early due to emotion
- Consecutive losses of more than 2× normal loss day
- Losing more than 5% of account in a single day
- Feeling "out of control" in positions

## 16. Common Mistakes Catalog

### Mistake 1: Chasing Breakouts
**Wrong:** Enter as soon as price breaks above a level  
**Right:** Wait for the pullback retest, then enter at the level  
> *"By the time the trade looks good, someone like me will be exiting as you are entering."*

### Mistake 2: No Stop Loss Pre-Defined
**Wrong:** Decide stop loss after entering  
**Right:** Define stop before entry; it gives you confidence to enter  

### Mistake 3: Adding to Losers
**Wrong:** Averaging down into a failing position  
**Right:** Exit at invalidation level; re-enter fresh if setup re-establishes  

### Mistake 4: Ignoring Macro Levels
**Wrong:** Trading a good-looking short-term setup under a major daily level  
**Right:** Always zoom out first; a major daily/weekly level nearby invalidates short-term entries  

### Mistake 5: Trading the NTZ
**Wrong:** Trading back and forth inside pre-market range all day  
**Right:** Wait for price to exit NTZ with a confirmed B&R  

### Mistake 6: Wrong Market Context
**Wrong:** Using B&R (a continuation strategy) during a consolidating market  
**Right:** Reduce size in consolidation; switch to smaller targets or sit out  

### Mistake 7: Tunnel Vision on Short-Term Levels
**Example:** Palanteer — took a short-term B&R long at $111, while the MAJOR level was $112 earnings low acting as resistance. Lost 37%.  
**Rule:** Always check the 1H and 4H chart for nearby major levels before entering on a 2-min signal.

### Mistake 8: Waiting for Certainty
> *"We confuse confirmation with certainty. Certainty does not exist on the stock market. If you are waiting for certainty in your entry points, you are already behind the ball."*  
**Right:** Accept uncertainty; trust the system; enter in the battle zone.
